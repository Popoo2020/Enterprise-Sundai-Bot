EVIDENCE & TRUST

Precise claims are part of responsible AI.

SundAI separates verified facts, source-backed guidance, professional interpretation, illustrative examples and commercial availability so readers can see what is documented, what is guidance and what remains context-dependent.

CLAIM LABELS

What each type of statement means.

Verified fact

Documented public information

Company registration, publication dates, official regulatory timelines and public technical documentation are linked to the underlying source whenever practical.

Source-backed guidance

Interpretation with citations

Guides explain how official requirements and frameworks can translate into operational decisions. They are not presented as regulator, standards-body or legal advice.

Illustrative

Delivery scenarios

Examples show how an engagement could be structured. They are explicitly not named client references, completed engagements or guaranteed outcomes.

Measured outcome

Published only with evidence

Performance, savings, adoption, security or risk-reduction claims should be published only when there is a defined baseline, measurement method and permission to disclose the result.

Relationship

Partners are not inferred

Using a technology platform, holding a certification, attending a programme or linking to another organisation does not by itself mean a formal partnership.

Certification

Scope matters

SundAI does not describe the organisation as ISO-certified unless a current certificate with a verifiable scope is published. Individual education and organisational certification are kept distinct.

Availability

Published options are not current engagements

A service page or indicative price describes a possible organisation-level commercial format. It does not by itself establish a current client engagement, immediate availability, reserved capacity, a fixed staffing model, personal delivery by a named individual, hours worked, client volume or revenue.

EVIDENCE HIERARCHY

Primary sources first, interpretation second.

01

Primary authority

Legislation, regulators, standards bodies, official registries and first-party technical documentation.

02

Direct project evidence

Approved deliverables, test records, measurements, contracts, source code and other first-party artifacts where publication is permitted.

03

Secondary context

Reputable research and analysis can add context, but should not replace a primary source for a legal or standards claim.

04

SundAI interpretation

Practical recommendations are labelled and should be tested against the organisation’s role, system, sector and applicable law.

SERVICE AVAILABILITY

Availability is confirmed only when an engagement is agreed.

Commercial accuracy: Scope, contributors, delivery model, timetable and capacity are confirmed separately for each enquiry before work is accepted. A public service description is not evidence that work has been sold, started, delivered or personally undertaken by any named person.

WHAT SUNDAI DOES NOT CLAIM

Trust is also defined by what is left unsaid.

No invented clients or testimonials

Client names, logos, quotes and project results are not published without a factual basis and appropriate permission.

No automatic compliance guarantee

Templates, checklists and framework mappings can support compliance work but do not prove that a specific AI system or organisation complies with law.

No implied regulator or vendor endorsement

Citing official sources or working with a platform does not mean the regulator, standards body or vendor endorses SundAI.

No inference of individual working time or availability

An organisational role, publication, service page, price or enquiry mechanism does not by itself state a person's working time, personal availability or amount of work performed.
