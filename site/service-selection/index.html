Three decisions that make the brief clearer

Finding knowledge or completing work?

Choose a private assistant and RAG pilot when authorised users need answers grounded in your documents. Choose secure automation when a defined event should move information or complete a process across systems. Retrieval and actions can be combined, but each action, permission and approval must be scoped.

Understanding requirements or testing an attack path?

Choose governance for applicable requirements, evidence gaps, responsibilities and a readiness plan. Choose a pentest to investigate authorised technical targets and prioritise fixes. A pentest is useful evidence; it does not replace a governance programme or establish compliance.

Protecting releases or controlling cloud access?

Choose DevSecOps for repository checks, pipeline identities, release gates and recovery guidance. Choose cloud security for selected identities, resources, configuration and audit signals in one account or project. Agree the handoff if a deployment pipeline also needs cloud permissions changed.

Five things to agree before work starts

Bring the answers you have. Unanswered questions are part of the discovery conversation, not a reason to write a vague contract.

  1. 01

    A written scope with limits

    Name the process, product, repository or cloud account; list targets, interfaces, environments and exclusions. Record deliverables, refinement or retest limits and how additional work is approved.

  2. 02

    Suppliers and the complete data path

    Identify what data enters each provider, model endpoint, knowledge store and log. Agree access, supplier data-use terms, hosting region, retention and deletion. European service delivery does not by itself establish where every supplier processes data.

  3. 03

    Owners who can approve decisions

    Name the business owner, technical contact, change approver and risk decision-maker. Provide authorised access and a contact for stopping tests or handling failures. Define which actions need human approval.

  4. 04

    Acceptance, failure behaviour and running costs

    Choose representative inputs and observable checks. Agree what happens with unavailable systems, denied access, uncertain answers or repeated events. Record cost estimates, budgets or caps, external licences and the person who approves changes.

  5. 05

    A handover your team can operate

    Agree which configuration records, test evidence, runbooks and access-transfer steps your team receives. Record remaining risks, maintenance ownership and separately required support. An entry project does not automatically include continuous monitoring or an availability SLA.

Make acceptance observable

A general promise to be secure or useful is hard to assess. Select checks tied to the agreed scope, representative data and your operating constraints. The examples below can start that discussion.

Compliance, GRC & AI Governance

  • Each agreed control area links to available evidence or a recorded gap, with a proposed owner and next step.
  • Document drafts identify the agreed process and framework, assumptions and decisions needing your approval.

Secure AI Business Automation

  • Replaying an agreed input does not create a duplicate action; the test records the outcome.
  • An agreed downstream failure produces the defined alert or human handoff; a sensitive action waits for required approval.

Private AI Assistants, Agents & RAG

  • The agreed restricted-document test denies retrieval and source access to a role without permission.
  • Agreed supported questions cite permitted sources; an unsupported question produces the defined refusal or handoff.

OSINT, Pentesting & Red Teaming

  • Any reported finding records an authorised target, proportionate reproduction evidence, severity and remediation guidance.
  • The agreed verification records the status of included fixes within the authorised scope and the package’s 30-day window.

DevSecOps & Secure Delivery Pipelines

  • An agreed test change exercises a release gate and records the expected block, approval or authorised exception.
  • Deployment identity permissions and the documented rollback steps are reviewed against the agreed target and environments.

Cloud Security Engineering, Automation & Maintenance

  • An agreed role change is checked for authorised access and excessive permissions before approved activation.
  • An agreed test event reaches the designated audit log or alert route; emergency-access instructions identify ownership and approval.
